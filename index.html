The Silent Threat Beneath Our Oceans

As early as 1910, the dumping of chemical and conventional munitions was recorded in the English Channel. This process continued throughout all seas as a cheap means of disposal for militaries until the mid-1970s when countries began to understand the environmental catastrophe they were creating.

1.6 million tons of underwater munitions—chemical, biological, and conventional—are rusting in 54 seas and oceans. Earth's five major ocean gyres circulate water and contaminants across all hemispheres. These slow-moving conveyor belts do not respect borders.

Evidence shows cancer rates up to 5x higher in coastal populations near known munitions sites. Shellfish toxicity linked to mustard gas and lewisite degradation has been documented. TNT with a 5,000-year half-life bioaccumulates in fish and coral.

Chemical Contamination
Munitions' chemical breakdown is spreading deadly arsenic, mustard gas compounds, and lewisite degradation products across the sea floor and into the food chain.
Unstable Ordnance
Unexploded ordnance is becoming more unstable with time, posing acute risks to fishing fleets, offshore energy infrastructure, and submarine cables.
Marine Ecosystem Collapse
Bioaccumulation in fish, coral bleaching, and dead zones near dump sites threaten food security for billions of people who depend on healthy oceans.
Ocean Gyre Dispersion
There is no "away" on Spaceship Earth. Contaminants from one region propagate through global ocean circulation, affecting ecosystems thousands of miles away.
Radiological Hazards
Radiological weapons dumped at sea compound the crisis, intersecting with radioactive water releases and creating compound stressors on already-stressed ecosystems.

Global Authority on Underwater & Submerged Munitions

Terrance (Terry) P. Long is the Chair and founder of the International Dialogue on Underwater Munitions (IDUM) and a world-leading expert on sea-dumped chemical and conventional weapons. A retired Canadian Military Engineer with over 30 years of experience specializing in Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD), Terrance has led international efforts to address the environmental and human health impacts of underwater and submerged munitions globally.

Terrance P. Long is a recognized global authority and leading voice in the response to underwater munitions. As the driving force behind UN Ocean Action #21356, he pioneered an international dialogue to address the environmental and safety risks of sea-dumped weapons. Through decades of dedicated advocacy, Terry has collaborated with international bodies, research institutes, and Baltic Sea regional initiatives to shape global models for marine protection and munitions clearance. His leadership within the International Dialogue on Underwater Munitions (IDUM) continues to unite governments, scientists, and industry stakeholders, leveraging critical mapping data and risk assessments to drive safer, more sustainable ocean cleanup operations worldwide.

International Research Initiatives

A shallow underwater munitions site offshore Nova Scotia, Canada
2011–2014 | Baltic Sea · Gotland & Gdańsk Deep
Chemical Munitions Search & Assessment

A €4.5M project, part-financed by the European Union through the European Regional Development Fund (Baltic Sea Region Programme 2007–2013), that mapped the Gotland and Gdańsk Deep chemical weapons dumpsites, assessed their toxicity to aquatic life, modelled leakage, and produced guidelines for managing contaminated sediments.

IDUM role: Partner and Co-Director (Terrance P. Long)
Underwater chemical munitions site in the Polish Economic Zone
2013–2016 | Baltic Sea
Monitoring of Dumped Munitions

A North Atlantic Treaty Organization Science for Peace and Security (NATO SPS) project that established a monitoring network for chemical weapons dumpsites in the Baltic Sea, using Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) and Remotely Operated Vehicles (ROVs), with habitat status evaluation, fish health studies and modelling across three representative areas.

Lead partner: Institute of Oceanology, Polish Academy of Sciences · IDUM role: Partner and Co-Director (Terrance P. Long)
2016–2019 | Baltic Sea
Decision Aid for Marine Munitions

An EU Interreg Baltic Sea Region project (European Regional Development Fund, EU Strategy for the Baltic Sea Region) that built decision-support software to help maritime, defence and environmental administrations manage dumped chemical and conventional munitions: evaluating the risks of individual munitions, categorising threats and assessing remediation methods. Study areas included the Gulf of Finland, the Bornholm and Gdańsk Deeps, the Little Belt and the Skagerrak.

Lead partner: Chalmers University of Technology · IDUM role: Partner and Co-Director (Terrance P. Long)

International Scientific and Technology Advisory Board

Chaired by Terry, the International Scientific and Technology Advisory Board (ISTAB) on Underwater Munitions consists of diplomats, scientists, academics, and munitions experts representing more than 10 nationalities. It provides independent advice on scientific, technical, and organizational aspects in response to sea-dumped munitions, and promotes dialogue and multilateral contacts among interested governments, international organizations, academic institutions, and the global community.

The ISTAB was originally appointed to IDUM at the request of the Foreign Minister of Lithuania during the 2010 Conference of States Parties of the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons (OPCW). Members work in their personal capacity and meet twice annually in The Hague, The Netherlands.
